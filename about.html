The problem

We have more films to choose from than ever, and we still spend twenty minutes choosing. Knowing you want "a thriller" doesn't help much — what you actually want is something that fits tonight: your mood, who's on the sofa, and how long you've got.

Long recommendation lists make this worse. Forty suggestions is the same problem in a new shape.

What FilmMatch does

It asks four questions — your mood, who you're watching with, your age, and how much time you have. Then it gives you four films. Not forty.

Each one comes with a poster, a short description with no spoilers, and one sentence saying why it picked that film for your answers. Tap it and you go straight to where you can watch it.

You can use all of it without an account. Signing in adds one thing: mark a film Seen or Not for me and it stops coming back.

Three decisions I'd defend

Four films, not a list

The first result is deliberately the biggest thing on the screen, so the app actually answers the question instead of handing the decision back. If the top pick is wrong that's a louder mistake — but the whole promise was fewer choices.

It explains itself, honestly

Every "why this one" line is built from the real reasons the film was chosen, so it can never overclaim. Films the app can't justify don't get a reason invented for them.

Half the films are ones you haven't heard of

Otherwise you get the same famous titles every time. But never more than two out of four — four unknown films feels like the app is dodging the question rather than curating.

What I set up

The whole thing runs on services I configured myself:

  • Google sign-in — set up in Google Cloud and connected to the app's login.
  • Supabase — the database, its security rules, and the login provider.
  • Vercel — hosting, plus analytics so I can see which moods and answers people actually pick.
  • GitHub — the code, and a monthly job that refreshes the film data on its own.

That monthly job checks its own work: it tries all 180 possible answer combinations and refuses to publish if anything looks wrong.

What's next
